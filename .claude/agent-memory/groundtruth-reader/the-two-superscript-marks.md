`1-043`'s `äˊᵋq-iˊmɪl` prints three glyphs in sequence: the vowel, then stress
U+02CA, then laryngeal U+1D4B, both stacked upper-right of the same vowel, **stress
first**. They are independent — the same page's `vaᵋy-qaˊa-raˊmkɪt` carries the
laryngeal alone on a bare `a`.

**The laryngeal is the mark most often misread as something uncatalogued.** On
`1-038` L37 it read as a superscript `g` — a shape not in the inventory anywhere —
because at a small scale its stacked open-e outline is just a rounded loop with a
tail, and "superscript g" sounds plausible next to a velar. R5 settles the
question before the raster does: an uncatalogued codepoint is far likelier a
misreading than the inventory being incomplete.

**How to apply:** an unfamiliar compound mark after a vowel is stress+laryngeal
until shown otherwise — check that reading before treating it as one unknown glyph.
The open curl distinguishes ᵋ from the plain stress tick at 4×, which is what 4× is
for. If it does not resolve there, it does not resolve: that is the finding, and it
goes in `note`.

A same-root contrast on the same page is worth more than more magnification.
`1-038` L35 has `Mêˊlhɪ-taˊn˙ñɪtan` where L37 has `Mêˊlhɪ-t-taˊn˙ñɪtan` — the mark
present in one spelling and absent in the other, matching the text's own
parenthetical gloss, which settles that it is meaningful rather than print noise.
That contrast cost no image at all.

**"Stress first" is not a fixed rule — it was one word's print order.** `1-100`'s
reindeer-color list has a recurring root (`Iᵋˊpɪrg̣ɪn`, `Uuᵋplɪˊlᴇ`, `Iᵋplɪˊlᴇ`,
`Ilh-ɪᵋplɪˊlᴇ`) where the laryngeal sits immediately after the word's first vowel
and the stress tick shows up later — sometimes right next to the laryngeal
(loop-then-tick, both directly after the initial vowel), sometimes several letters
downstream (after `plɪ`, right before a final `-lᴇ` suffix). Read the print order
as printed rather than assuming stress always precedes laryngeal; when the two
marks are adjacent, key them in the visual left-to-right order and flag the
placement in `note` rather than silently reordering to match `1-043`'s example.
