The medial-stem appendix prints three things per row: a stem, a participle
containing that stem, and a `см.` target naming the initial form. That looks
like three witnesses, and it is not. **You keyed all three.** A misreading you
carry across the row makes the row *more* consistent, not less.

Folio 188 is the case. `-нвилыткув-/-нвэлытков-` was keyed with `о` in the first
grade, and the participle and target were keyed `о` to match — three cells,
perfectly self-consistent, and the harmony alternation the entry exists to
record silently gone. Nothing internal could see it. The body's
`рывилыткувык`, keyed months earlier by a reader who never saw the page, is what
found it.

So when the fields of a row *disagree*, that is the row working as designed and
you should key what each one shows. When they *agree*, that is not
confirmation — it is one reading written three times.

**The corollary for adjudication: what settles a contradiction depends on
whether an outside witness exists.** Eleven `см.` targets in this appendix were
corrected against the body, because the body is a different reader. Thirty-nine
stem-vs-participle contradictions were left exactly as printed, because both
witnesses against the stem sit on the same line in the same hand. Same shape of
evidence, opposite outcome, and the difference is provenance rather than
strength of feeling. See [[what-settles-a-letterform]] for the failure this
avoids — an adjudicator at 4x has produced confident wrong readings three times
and corrections none.
