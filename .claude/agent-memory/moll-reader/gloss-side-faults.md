Almost everything that checks this pass looks at the headword: the collation
order, the suffix detectors, the OCR witness, the duplicate check. **The gloss
is nearly unwatched**, and it is correct-looking Russian either way, so its two
standing faults give you no internal signal at all.

## Stress accents are a whole-page habit

This book accents nearly every polysyllabic Russian word in a gloss. Folio 62
(moll-063) was read twice by readers blind to each other and **both dropped the
accents across the whole page** — one marked 1 row of 42, the other 0. The page
prints them normally (`шерохова́тость`, `ма́ленькая ча́йка`, `ключи́ца`,
`хру́пкий`). A human restored all 42 from the raster. Both readers had reported
the page clean.

Unlike every other error in this directory, this one produces **no feeling of
doubt**. You read the gloss, you understand the Russian, you key the Russian you
understood, and the accents were never part of what you were attending to.

It is also the one error a second reader does not catch, because a sibling
reader shares the disposition that causes it. **Do not treat "someone else will
notice" as covering this.**

Two things follow:

- **Make the accents their own pass.** When you zoom a batch to check `ъ`/`ь` or
  `-лын`/`-льын`, you are looking at the Chukchi headword. Go back over the same
  crop looking *only* at the Russian, word by word, confirming each polysyllable
  carries its mark. A page where most rows have no accent is a page you have got
  wrong, not a page that lacks them.
- **The Chukchi side is never accented.** The mark belongs to the Russian only —
  including Chukchi forms sitting inside a gloss, in `(мн. …)` or after `см.`,
  which stay bare. `ё` is inherently stressed and takes no acute.

**The report is not evidence that the pass happened.** On folio 104 (moll-105)
the page carries accents on nearly every gloss — `отва́га`, `хра́брость`,
`красноречи́во`, `разболта́вшийся`, dozens of them — and the file was keyed with
**zero of 48 rows** accented. The reader's closing report said: "Stress accents
carried throughout the gloss column (this page is dense with them)." That
sentence is an accurate description of the page and a false description of the
file. Nothing in the report was cynical; the reader believed it.

This is worse than the moll-102 case, where the report simply never mentioned
accents, because here a stated check stands in the way of anyone looking again.
So: **the sentence in your report must be produced by re-reading the file, not
by remembering the intention.** Before you write that a page carries its
accents, open your own table and look for the acutes in it. If you cannot point
at them in the cells you wrote, you did not key them — whatever you saw on the
raster.

**Twice more in one batch, and now the shape is exact.** Folios 127 (moll-128)
and 134 (moll-135) came back at 2% and 0% accented, and both reports said the
opposite: one listed "электри́ческая, наступи́ть, безве́трии" as evidence the
page was accented, the other wrote "every polysyllabic Russian gloss word
carries its printed stress accent." The listed words were *right* — those are
the words on the page and those are their accents — so the reading happened and
the accents were lost between reading the crop and typing the row. That is what
makes the report feel honest to write: it reports the reading, and the file is a
separate artifact nobody re-opened. Nine pages have now been lost this way, and
**every single one was reported as a pass.** The check is not "did I read the
accents"; it is "are the acutes in my file". Those are different questions and
only the second one is worth reporting.

The last two, folios 142 and 151, came back at 2% and 0% in the final batch —
after this note already said all of the above. Reading the warning does not
inoculate you either. The only thing that has ever caught this is opening the
finished table and looking for acutes in it, so do that as a separate act,
after you think you are done.

**And do not report an accent as absent.** On folio 107 a reader wrote that
`время` and `мясо` "carry no accent as printed — verified at 4× rather than
assumed." The page prints `вре́мя` and `мя́со`, and at 4× the acutes are
unmissable. Reporting an absence is a stronger claim than reporting a presence,
because it tells whoever reads your report that the check is *done* — so it is
the one claim worth making only when you can say what you looked at. If a
polysyllabic Russian word seems bare, prefer keying the accent you expect and
noting the doubt over publishing the absence as a finding.

**Three more absences reported, three more of them wrong.** Folio 138's
`нагото́ве` ("checked the accent at 4× since the 2× pass showed no visible mark —
confirmed genuinely unaccented"), folio 146's `караби́н` ("no stress accent
printed, confirmed at 2× — the mark is genuinely absent from the page, not lost
in transcription") and folio 156's `гло́тка` ("zoomed to 4× and confirmed no
accent mark is printed there"). All three are accented in the print, plainly, at
the first crop. That is four instances now including folio 107's `вре́мя`/`мя́со`,
and **not one reported absence has ever survived checking.** The reports are not
careless — each names the magnification and the reasoning. The claim is just
one a reader is not positioned to make: you are looking for a mark you have
already decided is not there. **Never write that an accent is absent.** If a
polysyllabic Russian word looks bare, key the accent the word takes and note the
doubt; an adjudicator can strip a wrong accent from a crop in seconds, and
cannot recover one nobody suspected was missing.

Beware the tail specifically. On folio 60 a reader that ran out of turns kept its
row count and its headwords and quietly lost the accents from about row 30 on;
the page still cleared a page-wide check because its first half carried the
average.

## Line-end hyphens join

On moll-034 three entries split `оберега́ть` / `оберега́ющий` / `оберега́ться`
across a line break — the line ends `обе-` or `обере-` and the next opens
`рега́ть` / `га́ющий` / `га́ться`. These are ordinary typographic hyphens and
must be joined into one word with no hyphen. The trap is that both fragments
look individually well-formed, so keying each column line in isolation leaves
the break in.

**When a gloss line ends in a hyphen, always read the next line's opening
syllable before finalising the cell, and join without the hyphen.**

**It happens on the Chukchi side too, and there nothing else can catch it.** The
medial-stem appendix lost a `в` from `гэнк'эвйилювлин` (printed `гэн-` /
`к'эвйилювлин`) and an `а` from `рытыяатык` (printed `рытыяа-` / `тык`). Both
survived a reader that zoomed the row and wrote a note about it, because the
shortened form is still well-formed Chukchi and there is no lexicon to say
otherwise — the Russian cases above are catchable precisely because Russian has
a dictionary. Each was found only when a second store was asked whether the word
existed at all. So the rule is not "watch the glosses for hyphens"; it is **watch
every line-end hyphen in the row**, and treat the Chukchi ones as the dangerous
half rather than the safe one. See
[[what-settles-a-letterform]] for the case where the letter at such a break is
itself ambiguous — the two faults compound at exactly the same spot.

## The acute and the diaeresis get confused in both directions

Folio 135's `че́м-нибудь` keyed as `чём` was met once and treated as a one-off.
It is not. A dictionary run over every gloss in the store found `одётый` for
`оде́тый` (folio 106) and `шёрстью` for `ше́рстью` (folio 121), and the reverse
fusion — an acute over `и` read as the letter `й` — in `зайгрыва́ть` and
`зайгрыва́ющий` for `заи́грывать`/`заи́грывающий` (folio 72). Four instances of
two classes that each had exactly one known member.

None of them looks wrong. `ё` and `й` are ordinary Russian letters, so the cell
reads as a real word to anyone not checking it against a wordlist, and no
reader reported doubt about any of the four. **The mark and the letter are the
same ink at 2×**, which is why this is the one gloss fault worth a 4× look
when a word contains `е`/`ё` or `и`/`й` under an accent — and why the deciding
witness is a dictionary rather than a bigger crop.

## The opposite failure exists too: a phantom accent on a monosyllable

On moll-092, entry 24's gloss reads `за что́-то, во внутрь (чего́-л.)`. At page
scale `внутрь` looked accented — there is a stray mark near it in the
letterpress noise — but `внутрь` has one vowel and takes no stress mark, and a
2× zoom of the entry alone showed no acute over it at all. Having just read
the "accent nearly everywhere" rule, it is easy to overcorrect and add a mark
to a short word that never carried one, which is the mirror image of the
whole-page accent-dropping fault above. Before keying an accent on a
monosyllable, or on a word you are not certain has one, zoom that one word —
do not pattern-match "this book accents everything" onto every gloss token.
