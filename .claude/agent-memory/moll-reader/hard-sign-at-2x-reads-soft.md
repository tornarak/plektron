On moll-102, entries 9–13 share the stem `пуур-`: `пууръу`, `пууръык`, `пууръын`,
`пууръыткульын`, `пууръэтык` — five headwords in a row, every one ending the
stem in `ъ` before the suffix. At 2x (both on the full marked page and in a
batched zoom crop) the sign read comfortably as `ь` in every one of the five,
with no felt doubt — the same shape [[lyn-lyyn-no-felt-doubt]] describes for
`-лын`/`-льын`. A 4x crop of the same five settled it immediately: flat top,
no bowl, a short tail breaking right at the baseline — `ъ`, not `ь`, in all
five.

**Why 2x is not enough here specifically:** the two signs differ in a small
structural detail (bowl vs. no bowl) rather than in the amount of ink present,
and at 2x on this face the difference sits below what the crop resolves,
matching [[what-settles-a-letterform]]'s standing case
(`ъ` against apostrophe-consonant resolves at 4x, not 2x).

**How to apply:** when several consecutive headwords share a stem ending in
what looks like `ь` or `ъ` before a suffix, treat the whole run as one
question and zoom it at 4x together, even if none of the five individually
felt doubtful. A stem that recurs five times is five chances for the same
misreading to go unflagged, since each one individually looks exactly as
confident as the last.
