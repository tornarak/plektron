On moll-129 entry 36, the full marked page read at 2x as `танн'ын'авэрыын` —
"platye, odezhda (zhenskaya)". Zoomed alone at 2x it is `танн'ын'авэръын`: a
hard sign, not a second `ы`, sits between `р` and the final `ын`. This is a
different substitution from [[lyn-lyyn-no-felt-doubt]] (a dropped `ь` before
`ы`) and from [[confusable-pairs-no-felt-doubt]]'s `ъ`/`ь` swap — here the
sign was not misread as another letter, it was absorbed into a repeated
vowel, so the word still looked perfectly well-formed as a run of `ы`.

**Why this is worth its own trigger:** neither existing rule fires on this
shape. `лы` isn't present, and there is no felt ambiguity between two
letterforms to compare — the eye simply reads "vowel, vowel" and moves on.

**How to apply: zoom on `ыы`, and on `ыы` only.** Not on doubled vowels in
general — that was this note's first wording and it is wrong. Counted across
the keyed corpus, doubled vowels are ordinary in this book: `аа` 96 headwords,
`ээ` 63, `ии` 56, `оо` 53, `уу` 33. Triggering on all of them buries the one
that means something. `ыы` is the outlier — **2 headwords in 5681 entries** —
so it is a cheap, near-zero-noise tell that a `ъ` sits in the middle of it and
was dropped.

**Both of those two were then checked, and one was this same error.** The
corpus held exactly `тыыркут` (real — its printed `(ед. тыырколгын)` carries
the same `ыы`, so the compositor is a witness for it) and `танн'эрыын` on
folio 128, five brackets from the entry above. The print reads `танн'эръын`.
So the reader that found this class made the same substitution twice on one
page, caught the second instance and not the first — which is the useful part:
**finding an error class does not inoculate you against it three lines later.**
Run the check over your whole finished table, not over the entry that prompted
it.
