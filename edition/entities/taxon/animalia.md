## gloss

A root, with [[taxon:plantae]], [[taxon:fungi]] and [[taxon:lichenes]]. **None
of the four is printed anywhere in the corpus**, and neither is any rank between
them and the genera — `Mammalia`, `Aves`, `Pisces`, `Insecta`, `Phocidae`,
`Anatidae`, `Gadidae`, `Diptera` are all absent from the text. They carry no
**Latin** alias row for that reason, which is `docs/edition.md`'s rule and not
this card's. Several of them do now carry an *English* row — `fish`, `mice`,
`eagle` — which is the same rule's other half rather than an exception to it: the
folk group that maps to no modern taxon is an alias on the coarsest honest rank,
and those words the book does print.
What they are is the reading view's spine — the species and
genera are a list, and the same set under a dozen names a reader recognizes is a
shape.

**A ligature was invisible to every report this store was built from**, which is
why five entries here came off the page rather than off a worklist: `Œstrus` and
`Œdemagena` (1-108), `Salmonidæ`, `chamæmorus`, and [[taxon:coregonidae]],
printed once at 1-188 and naming a fish family that changes where
[[taxon:coregonus]] sits. `lexicon misses` applied an ASCII shape floor to both
its arms; `--common` now runs `admit_foreign=True` and widens on *carries no
Tier-1 mark* instead. A Latin binomial is where this book keeps its ligatures,
so the hole was almost exactly taxon-shaped.

**The worked cases for the rank-admission rule**, which `docs/edition.md`
states: it admits [[taxon:salmonidae]] (both), [[taxon:gadidae]] (both, the
name aside), [[taxon:phocidae]] (three genera) and [[taxon:anatidae]] and
[[taxon:diptera]] (two each), and it refuses Cottidae, Canidae, Strigidae,
Scolopacidae, Oestridae and Tabanidae — one genus apiece, so the family would
add a level and no information. Those five genera hang from their class.
