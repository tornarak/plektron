## gloss

The **second** of 1-108's "two kinds of reindeer-flies", and the whole of what
the page says about which it is: "of smaller size and darker color", it "lays
its eggs in the reindeer's nostrils. The larvæ go up to the throat, and
penetrate the cartilage. The next year, when the maggots are full-grown, they
cause a constant cough, which continues until the last one drops to the ground."

The first fly is [[taxon:oestrus-tarandi]], whose maggot burrows into the back.

**Do not reconcile the two against modern usage here.** *Œdemagena tarandi* is
today's reindeer warble fly (*Hypoderma tarandi*) — the one of the *back* — and
the nose bot is *Cephenemyia trompe*, so Slunin's names as Bogoras carries them
sit opposite to the current ones. Which name belongs to which fly is a question
about the entomology of 1900 and about Slunin, not about this page, and the page
is unambiguous on the biology: this is the nostril fly. Recorded as printed.
