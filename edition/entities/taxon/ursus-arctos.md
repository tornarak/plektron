## gloss

The bear of the forest border — the one that kills a stray reindeer (1-109), that
the Reindeer Chukchee take in its den or with the spiked block laid on its path
(1-184, 1-113), that hibernates on the banks of the Lower Kolyma and was met
feeding on seaweed in Holy Cross Bay in June, 1901.

**The book calls this animal both *brown bear* and *black bear*, and they are one
species here.** It never sets the two words on the same page, never contrasts
them in a fauna list or a price list, and gives them one account between them.
The clearest witness is the bear ceremonial: 2-051 has the Lamut and Yukaghir
venerating the *black* bear, 2-136 has the Lamut boiling the head of the *brown*
bear whole and eating it at one sitting, and these are the same custom among the
same people. Chukchee draws no such line either — *Keiˊñin* is the word (2-197,
2-245), and it renders "Brown Bear River" at 1-184 and plain "Bear River" at
1-359.

Nor is there a second bear it could be: neither *U. americanus*, which is North
American, nor *U. thibetanus*, whose range stops far south of the Kolyma, reaches
the country described. Dark-coated brown bears do.

A bear the page leaves uncoloured stays on [[taxon:ursus]]; the sea bear is
[[taxon:ursus-maritimus]].
