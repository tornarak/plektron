{# The printed Contents as a locator, in the sidebar's column. **Not `_toc.html` narrowed.** That partial is the Contents *as a document* — 316 rows set the way the book sets them, leaf marks in the margin, the folio hard right. This is a different question asked of the same store: where am I, and what is around me. So the whole outline is served, all five levels of it, **collapsed** — and what stands open is the division you are in, unrolled whole. Collapsed because 316 rows in a 20rem column is a thing you have to read rather than a thing that tells you where you are; whole because a chapter opened to the one row that matched shows you the row you already had and hides its neighbours, which is the opposite of a locator. The leaf marks are gone: where the printed Contents happened to turn a page is a fact about the binding of the front matter, and it has nothing to say about where you are in chapter XII. **`
` and not a script**, for the map tree's reason: disclosure is what the element is, and a panel that needed JS to open a chapter would be a panel that does not open when the JS fails. **Served once, unmarked** (`/partial/toc`), because it is the same on every page and was ~77 KB of each. Nothing here is open, marked or counted: a page ships its marks as ids (`_toc_panel`, `data-marks` in `_sidebar.html`) and `sidebar.js`, having fetched this, sets `.here`, `open` and the counts from them. The rule for which rows those are is the route's, not the script's. **No ids.** `_toc.html` gives every row its `toc-{id}` anchor, and on the Contents run this panel renders beside it — a second copy of those ids on one page, which is the one thing an id may not be. Nothing links into this panel. `data-toc` names the entry, which is how `sidebar.js` finds the row a mark names and the row for the heading being read. Row classes are `_toc.html`'s — `.toc-title`, `.toc-num`, `.page-no` — so the two renderings of one store share their vocabulary and their link rule: live only where a witness places the folio, and where it does not, the title is not a link either. #} {# The row's own contents, which are the same whether or not it has children.
