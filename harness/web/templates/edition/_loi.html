{# The printed List of Illustrations, as one list across its six leaves: figures then plates, in the store's own order, which is the book's. Two links, and each is a store already in the tree answering a question the printed list poses: the folio through the folio index, and the figure number through `figures.by_number`. **A number that resolves to more than one zone is printed plain**, never guessed — the book restarts figure numbering at 1 on every plate, so 1-4 are genuinely ambiguous, and 240 is the corpus's one unpaired caption. 297 of 302 resolve; the other five are where a link would be an invention. **The whole entry is the link, and it opens a card in the stack.** A reader reaches for the caption, not for the numeral, so the caption is the target and is drawn as if it were not a link — `_toc.html`'s reasoning. What it opens is the stack rather than the lightbox, which is the opposite of the rule everywhere else in this edition and for that rule's own reason: a picture goes to the lightbox because it is *already on the page*, and here the page is a catalogue that names it. See `openFigure`. **No thumbnails.** They were tried and taken out: 294 pictures down the margin turned a list you read into a contact sheet you scroll, and the printed list is a list. The card is where a picture belongs here — one at a time, beside the entry that sent for it. The numerals stay live too and open a card for their own picture alone, which is the only thing that answers a three-figure entry: with several crops no one of them is *the* picture. The `href` is a real crop URL throughout, so this works with no JS and a middle-click still opens the file. #}
