## summary

Bogoras describes the people of this book in the vocabulary of nineteenth-century
physical anthropology — cephalic indices, faces "strongly Mongolian in outline",
bodies measured against those of "Caucasians". **That framework is not a
superseded answer so much as a superseded question.** The traits it measured do
not partition human populations into types, and where northeast Siberians came
from is now read from genomes and from linguistic reconstruction instead: see
[[sikora-2019]] and [[flegontov-2019]].

Worth knowing before reading chapter II as its author's racial theory: **it is
not one.** The [[event:jesup-expedition]] was designed and directed by
[[person:boas]], whose programme set historical contact against race as the
explanation of why peoples resemble each other, and Bogoras's comparisons here
run consistently in one direction — narrowing the distance between the Chukchee
and Europeans rather than widening it.

## full

### What the book is doing

Chapter II opens on the physical type of the Chukchee, and 1-055 gives the method
outright: "As ascertained from anthropological measurements", followed by a
cephalic index. The measurements were part of the [[event:jesup-expedition]]'s
brief, and 2-073 records what taking them was actually like in the field.

The categories Bogoras reaches for are [[people:mongoloids]] and
[[people:caucasoids]], and the entries for both, with
[[people:physical-anthropology]], say what this edition does and does not claim
by recording the words at all.

Two of the book's uses are worse than merely dated. 3-060 explains the
childlessness of Russian–Chukchee marriages on the Kolyma by "the exhaustion of
vital force", with the scarcity of "blood really Russian and Caucasian" as the
reason; that is degeneration theory, not observation. 3-093 calls a man "a
specimen of some lower type of man". Both are worth reading as evidence about
their author and his period, and neither is evidence about anybody described.

### Modern Follow-Up

[[sikora-2019]] sequenced ancient genomes from northeastern Siberia and
described the deep population history of the region, and [[flegontov-2019]]
traced Palaeo-Eskimo ancestry into Chukotka and North America — including into
speakers of the Chukotko-Kamchatkan languages, which is where the genetic and
the linguistic questions meet. [[glottolog-ckt]] is the current classification
of that family, to which Chukchi belongs.

Two things are worth holding onto from the contrast. The modern account is about
**populations and their histories of movement and contact**, not about types a
face can be sorted into; and it reaches conclusions Bogoras's own framework had
no way to state, while confirming the thing his fieldwork was actually organised
around — that the peoples on the two sides of the Bering Strait are connected by
history.
