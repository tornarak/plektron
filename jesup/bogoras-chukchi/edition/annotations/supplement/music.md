## summary

Bogoras transcribes songs and describes drumming he had no way to record. 1-350
gives "The Song of the Fox", a dialogue between the fox and the bear sung in two
voices — "the fox speaks in a thin treble voice; the bear" in another — and
2-166 reports that in imitative song "the walrus and the bear roar, the reindeer
snorts". A page of text cannot carry either.

Two published collections can. [[melodiya-1974]] is field recording from both
peoples of the book's own coast. [[kolyma]] is Henri Lecomte's **Sibérie 3**,
made in November 1992 in four [[river:kolyma]] villages, and its middle stretch is the
closest thing on record to what 2-166 describes: [[t27]] and [[t28]] are grebe,
crane and barnacle goose, [[t29]] a partridge, [[t30]] a gull. The long text
below is the record's apparatus, singer by singer.

## full

### The record

**Kolyma: chants de nature et d'animaux / Songs of nature and animals.** Buda
Musique, *Musique du Monde*, 92566-2, "Sibérie 3", subtitled *Čukč · Even ·
Jukaghir*. Production, recordings, photographs and texts by **Henri Lecomte**;
Collection Dominique Buscail, directed by Gilles Fruchaux. 43 tracks, 1 h 02′
53″. Recorded in November 1992 in four villages of the lower Kolyma — Kolymskoe,
Čerskij, Andrjuškino and Nelemnoe — most of them north of the Arctic Circle.

Three peoples: the [[people:reindeer-chukchi]] of the Kolyma,
the [[people:even]]s, "once named Lamuts",
and the [[people:yukaghir]].

Lecomte notes that the villages are mixed,
that everyone speaks [[people:yakut]] and Russian,
and that the national languages are in difficulty.

### Genres, as the booklet sets them out

Six, of which three matter here. Everyday, ritual, lyrical and personal songs —
*ike*, *iken*, *yakhte*. **Imitation of bird and animal cries**, tied to birdcall
hunting. And throat rasping on the breath drawn in and out, the basis of the Even
*seedie* and Yukaghir *londol* dances: the Chukchi call it *pič eynen*, the
Yukaghir *tumun khontol*. Round dances, drum music and heroic epic make up the
rest.

Chukchi songs are described as slow and lyrical, built on a repeated theme
ornamented with tremolo or staccato and broken by an almost imperceptible breath
mid-phrase. Even songs use a more natural voice and fall into couplets, which
lets a singer recover breath; each singer has their own syllables carried over
from shamanic ceremony.

### Čukč

**Anna Alexandrovna Kaulgina** (*Kerginan*, "light"), born August 1943 in the
tundra, reindeer breeder. Kolymskoe, 11 November 1992. — [[t01]], [[t02]] (a
wordless song), [[t03]].

**Nikolaï Andreevič Dečkov** (*Peneteun*), born late summer 1929 in the Khalerča
tundra, reindeer breeder. Kolymskoe, 12 November 1992. — [[t04]], [[t05]],
[[t06]].

**Innokenti Jakovlevič Goruline** (*Turkekeu*), born in Khalerča, master breeder.
Kolymskoe, 12 November 1992. — [[t07]]. He is **Even and sings only in Chukchi**,
which the booklet offers as its example of what the mixed villages do to a
repertoire.

**Ivan Ilijč Čymkil**, born 1 January 1927 in Kolymskoe, head breeder. Kolymskoe,
12 November 1992. — [[t08]], improvised on the spot for the recordist and his
guide; [[t09]]; [[t10]].

**Slava Egorovič Kemlil** (*Keul*, "born in the tundra"), born 8 August 1963 in
the Khalerča tundra, zoo technician. Čerskij, 16 November 1992. — [[t11]],
wordless, over the single-membrane frame drum *jarakh*; [[t12]]. His
improvisations run in three parts: imitation of animal or bird cries with the
drum, recreating the atmosphere of the former shamanic *kamlanye*; then throat
rasping, *pič eynen*; then the reindeer's cry.

**Egor Andreevič Nutendli** (*Ruthlygin*), born 31 December 1933 in the Khalerča
tundra, head breeder, and Slava Kemlil's father. Čerskij, 16 November 1992. —
[[t13]]; [[t14]], which the booklet places halfway between singing and speech and
which remembers his mother's herds before collectivization.

### Jukaghir

Songs of the **Oduls**, the Yukaghir of the High Kolyma, gathered in the village
of Andrjuškino — a people once of twelve tribes, and 400 souls at the 1979
census. The booklet describes the voice as natural and close to the Even manner.

**Matriona Nikolaevna Toxtossa**, born Kourilova (*Eguedie*), born 12 March 1941
at Toustakh-Seign, seamstress. Andrjuškino, 10 November 1992. — [[t32]], the
gulls' song, whose refrain is *ij je juo*; [[t33]], a welcome to guests, on the
*deretii* model of the Sakha *tojuk*.

**Varvara Andreevna Slepsova**, born 10 December 1928 near the Olera, reindeer
breeder (father Yukaghir, mother Even). Andrjuškino, 11 November 1992. —
[[t34]].

**Ekaterina Ivanova Tymkil** (*Mokoe*, "the small one"), born 15 May 1921 near
the Olera, seamstress. Kolymskoe, 12 November 1992. — [[t35]], [[t36]] (on the
old *olonkho*, the Sakha epic), [[t37]], [[t38]], and [[t39]], where she is the
only Yukaghir singer on the record to use *tumun khontol* throat clearing to
imitate the reindeer's cry, in the *seedi* dance. Besides Yukaghir she speaks
Even, Chukchi, Sakha and Russian.

**Nikolaï Nikolaïevič Deškof** (*Tokhuian*), born 7 November 1912 in the Prorva
region, hunter. Nelemnoe, 19 November 1992. — [[t40]], [[t41]], [[t42]]. He and
the singer after him sing in *wadul*, the southern Yukaghir dialect.

**Akulina Vassilevna Slepsova**, born 26 June 1930 in the taiga near the
Nugundannoe, seamstress. Nelemnoe, 19 November 1992. — [[t43]].

### What it is worth to this book

The animal imitations are the direct match. 2-166 says the walrus and the bear
roar and the reindeer snorts; Bogoras describes imitative singing repeatedly and
could record none of it. [[t27]] to [[t31]] are that genre, from the same tundra,
ninety years later.

The drum is the second. 1-350 has the two-voice fox-and-bear dialogue, and the
chapter on games and songs describes drumming throughout; [[t11]] is a wordless
song over the *jarakh*, and Kemlil's three-part improvisation is said outright to
recreate the atmosphere of the shamanic *kamlanye* that part 2 is largely about.

The third is not a match but a measure. [[t14]] remembers herds seized at
collectivization, thirty years after Bogoras counted them; [[t19]] is an Even
woman remembering a Chukchi aunt; [[t07]] is an Even man who sings only in
Chukchi. The book treats these peoples as distinct, and the recordings are what
the same country sounded like after a century of mixing and Soviet settlement had
gone through it.
