## gloss

Both mentions are the same motif, and neither is natural history. chukchi-2-013: the
owner of game is a tiny being with a sledge of grass who "may drive a mouse or
a small root of *Polygonum viviparum*" — and **"The lemming is his polar bear.
He kills it, and loads it on his sledge."** chukchi-2-010 sets the inversion the other
way, an amulet-bear that loses "its former size, and the bear turns into a
lemming", which Bogoras then notes is "the same in regard to the owner of
game". The lemming is doing one job in this book: it is the smallest quadruped
a Chukchee reader would picture, and it is there to measure how small its owner
is. His name is left unspelled here because the store has no entry for him yet
and the transcription is not this entry's to fix.

**Why the entry stops at a subfamily.** *Lemming* names no clade. It is a grade
spread across three tribes of Arvicolinae — Lemmini (*Lemmus*, *Myopus*,
*Synaptomys*), Dicrostonychini (*Dicrostonyx*) and Lagurini — so even the tribe
is too narrow to hold the English word. Chukotka has lemmings from two of those
tribes at once, *Lemmus* and *Dicrostonyx torquatus*, and the two pages carry
nothing that could separate them: no colour, no season, no burrow, no coat.
Naming a genus here would be a claim about which lemming a storyteller had in
mind, which is not a thing the text records. Arvicolinae is the smallest node
that certainly contains it.

Distinct from [[taxon:rodentia]]'s `mice`, which stops higher for the opposite
reason — *mouse* in ordinary English is a grade of small rodent and gives up
almost nothing, where *lemming* names a real recognisable group and giving that
up would lose what the sentence is using.
