## gloss

The book's *red fox*, and it sets no binomial. chukchi-1-179 names the two foxes
together — "the most important are the white and the red fox" — and makes this
the scarcer: [[taxon:vulpes-lagopus]] is "much more numerous," while the red
"also lives all along the Arctic and Pacific coasts."

*Black fox*, *cross fox* and *gray fox* are this animal as well: colour morphs
the fur trade graded and priced apart, not species. chukchi-3-171's table sets one black
fox at twenty red and one gray at two, and chukchi-1-078's fair returns count all three
in one line. Which morph a given "gray fox" was is not recoverable from the
page and nothing here decides it — only that the animal under all four words is
one.

A reader should not read *gray fox* as the modern English one: that names
*Urocyon cinereoargenteus*, which is North American and does not occur in the
country the book describes.

Bare *fox* stays on [[taxon:vulpes]], which is where a mention that names no
colour belongs.
