## gloss

“Spotted taˊɪrg̣ɪn”: it “covers the skin with large spots of a light, ashen
color, often over the whole body, including the face and the hair of the head.
It does not seem to affect the health” (chukchi-1-065). A man named Tuˊrkew who had it
was called Keliˊlɪ-Tuˊrkew, “Spotted Tuˊrkew”, and was well known in the Anui
country for his piebald hair; at fifty he was “still active for his time of
life”.

**Identification: vitiligo.** Depigmented macules going confluent over the body,
health untouched, and white hair in the patches — the piebald head is the tell.

**The modifier is spelled twice and differently — Kalêˊlɪ- in the disease
name, Keliˊlɪ- in the man's — and that is vowel harmony, not an
inconsistency.** Chukchee harmony is dominant–recessive: a dominant morpheme
raises every recessive vowel in its word, i to e and e to a. The head noun
taˊɪrg̣ɪn is dominant, so the stem surfaces raised when compounded with it;
Tuˊrkew is recessive and leaves it alone. One word, harmonising to what it is
attached to.

The book names it under [[being:tairgin]], though the two are opposite processes;
see that entry.
