## gloss

The Raven of the tales and the incantations: the one who "restored to the earth
the sources of light" (chukchi-2-045), who is called on for pains in the stomach
(chukchi-2-233), who goes to the Pebbly River and asks his wives for news (chukchi-2-235), and
who runs after the children in the game at chukchi-1-351 while they shout that his eyes
are lean and theirs are fat. chukchi-1-352 notes the "numerous Raven legends which play
an important part" in the collection this book abridges, and chukchi-2-208 sets a "Raven
Incantation" apart from the others.

**Not the Creator, and not Koryak Big-Raven.** chukchi-2-041 is explicit on both counts:
among the Koryak the Creator "is often identified with the Big-Raven ... who
represents the chief Deity. In Chukchee tales, on the contrary, the Raven ...
even in his name, plays a less important part, and is almost always
distinguished from the Creator." Where he does loom larger — the Telqäˊp tundra
and Big-River camps — Bogoras reads Koryak influence. Big-Raven is a separate
figure under a separate name and wants a separate entry; this one does not claim
that form.

**And a distinction the book records without settling.** chukchi-2-045: one shaman
"distinguished between this Raven and the Raven who restored to the earth the
sources of light" — the first an "assistant" to the Creator or the Zenith, who
lives near the Polar Star and devours disease as a bird devours worms; the
second a "very mighty 'Being'" who dealt with mankind only at the creation and
afterwards "transformed himself into thunder, and became invisible." One
informant's ruling, reported here and not adopted.

**No native name is keyed here.** chukchi-2-041 and chukchi-2-045 give the Chukchee and Koryak
names of these figures, and chukchi-2-039 a niece's; every one of them reaches this entry
only through OCR, and a glyph read off a scan is a proposal until a human
ratifies it. The alias table claims the English word and nothing else.
