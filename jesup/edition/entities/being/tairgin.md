## gloss

“More uncommon is taˊɪrg̣ɪn, a peculiar disease that increases the pigment in the
skin, so that the afflicted look like mulattoes. They do not complain of any
pain, but lose in strength and agility; nor can they endure the hardships of
their former pursuits, though their term of life does not seem to be shortened.
I met with only five cases of this disease” (chukchi-1-064).

**Identification: unresolved; Addison's disease leads.** Generalised
hyperpigmentation with weakness and lost stamina, and no pain, is the shape of
adrenal insufficiency, which tuberculosis caused most of in this period. Against
it: untreated Addison's was fatal, usually within a few years, and Bogoras says
these lives were not shortened; it is rare, and five cases met by one traveller
is many; and the account names none of its other signs, wasting, stomach
trouble or a craving for salt. The book records consumption on St. Lawrence
Island (chukchi-1-234), not in Chukotka itself. Not adopted here.

The name is not a vaˊɪrg̣ɪn compound the way [[being:syphilis]]'s is — it shares
only the nominalising ending — and nothing here claims it is.
[[being:kaleli-tairgin]], which the book files under this name, runs the other
way and removes pigment; whether taˊɪrg̣ɪn is one disease or a native category
for a disturbance of skin colour in either direction is not settled by these
pages. Autoimmune Addison's and vitiligo often occur together, which would tie
the two clinically, but that is a fact about the diseases and says nothing of
how the Chukchee reckoned them.
