## gloss

“Of nervous diseases, which are also dreaded and considered ‘subject to
shunning,’ I may make mention of iteˊyun, akin to epilepsy” (chukchi-1-064). Sometimes
the usual fits; sometimes “convulsions or … contraction of the muscles,
amounting almost to a temporary catalepsy”, lasting several hours with the
patient unconscious throughout, passing off and returning within days in a more
violent form. “Iteˊyun progresses rapidly, and invariably ends in the death of
the sufferer”, though it may take years. With [[being:iumetun]] it is attributed
to special spirits whose names are not spoken, and a man taken ill with either
while travelling can expect no house, no fire and no warm food.

**Identification: unresolved.** Three candidates, and the account fits none of
them whole. Trichinellosis, from [[taxon:trichinella-nativa]] in walrus, seal and
bear meat, gives seizures in heavy infections, and a shared meal would make
clusters within a camp; but it runs its course in weeks to months and most
sufferers recover, which is neither the invariable death nor the years.
Alveolar echinococcosis, from *Echinococcus multilocularis* in fox and sledge
dog, is fatal in about nine cases in ten untreated, and over years; but it is a
disease of the liver, reaching the brain in perhaps one case in a hundred, and
the account names no other sign. Its focus across the strait on St. Lawrence
Island is documented; its presence in Chukotka in Bogoras's day is not
established here. Epilepsy itself, of whatever cause, is the comparison
Bogoras makes twice — here, and “violent madness similar to epilepsy”
(chukchi-2-147) — and recurrent fits with recovery between are its shape. The
name is also a spirit's (chukchi-2-019), and a spirit need not name one disease:
“progresses rapidly” beside “lasts for years” already reads as more than one
course. None is adopted here.
