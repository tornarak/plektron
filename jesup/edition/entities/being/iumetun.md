## gloss

“Another disease of a like character is iuˊmetun, a kind of violent nervous
affection, which comes on at night like nightmare. During an attack the breath
appears shortened, the blood rushes to the face, and sometimes the sufferer
chokes on the spot” (chukchi-1-064). Paired with [[being:iteyun]] as spirit-caused, its
name likewise not spoken, and shunned the same way.

**Identification: unresolved.** Three candidates, and the account does not choose
between them or exclude a combination. Seizures in sleep fit the most of it:
elsewhere the book ties iuˊmetun to “the much-dreaded fits and convulsions”
(chukchi-2-248), and calls it “of a like character” to iteˊyun, itself “akin to
epilepsy”. A seizure in sleep stops the breath or leaves it stertorous and
congests the face, and sudden death in epilepsy comes mostly at night, after
such a seizure. “Like nightmare” may be meant in the older sense of the word, a
weight on the chest and a sense of suffocation in sleep, which describes the
breath but kills no one. Sleep apnoea covers the nocturnal onset,
the shortened breath and the choking. Carbon-monoxide poisoning covers the
flushed face and the sudden death, and the sleeping-room is the setting for it —
a small closed skin chamber warmed and lit by a blubber lamp through the night;
but the spirit is one of the open country, waiting for travellers
(chukchi-2-019). Neither apnoea nor the poisoning gives convulsions. None is
adopted here.
