## gloss

The book's "Asiatic Eskimo" — the Yupik of the Asian shore, as against the
Alaskan varieties [[people:yupik]] is drawn from. Bogoras divides them on
chukchi-1-051 and chukchi-1-052 into three, and each division is a child or a member here: the
Aiˊwan of the Pacific coast, whose villages this entry names directly; the
[[people:naukans]] of East Cape; and the [[people:sireniks]] of Wuteˊen.

**Its extent is its villages, because no source draws it.** Asher 2007 carries the
Alaskan Yupik varieties and no
Asian one, "they are its comparanda, not substitutes for it". So this entry
names the settlements Bogoras assigns to it and the atlas frames on their union,
which claims a set of points rather than a boundary nobody drew.

**Membership is read off the ethnic blocks of chukchi-1-051, not off the names.** The
`ESKIMO.` heading opens at Wuteˊen and the Aiˊwan stretch closes at Nepeˊkuten,
"the last Aiˊwan village"; one of the villages between is mixed, Bogoras
putting Čeˊčin at "Chukchee and Aiˊwan, half and half", so [[place:tkachen]] is
named here and by [[people:maritime-chukchi]] both. An Eskimo *name* is not an Eskimo
village — [[place:enmilin]] carries one above the heading, and most of chukchi-1-052's
names north of Kihiˊni sit in a stretch he assigns to the Chukchee.

[[place:gambell]] is on [[land:st-lawrence-island]] and so outside the coastal
gazetteer's run; chukchi-1-164 is where the book names it.
