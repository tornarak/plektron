## gloss

**A holder for the book's racial vocabulary, not a people.** Chapter II opens on
the physical type of the Chukchee and measures it against two categories the
anthropometry of 1904 took as given: [[people:mongoloids]] and
[[people:caucasoids]]. They are here because the book uses the words and a reader
meeting them needs to know what they meant to the man writing, which is not what
they sound like now.

**They describe nothing.** Human variation is clinal — it grades continuously
across geography and does not partition into types — and the racial taxonomy
these terms belong to was abandoned by the discipline that produced them. Where
the book says a face is "strongly Mongolian in outline" (chukchi-1-056) it is reaching
for a type that was never there to reach for. Nothing in this store treats these
as classifications of anybody; they are read as period vocabulary, on the same
footing as an obsolete binomial.

**The book these words appear in was written against them.** The
[[event:jesup-expedition]] was designed and directed by [[person:boas]], whose
programme set historical contact against race as the explanation of why peoples
resemble each other; the expedition's whole design — survey both shores of the
North Pacific and look for what crossed — presupposes that culture travels rather
than inheres, and Boas spent the rest of his career turning that evidence against
racial determinism. So the vocabulary here is period furniture inside a project
aimed at knocking it down, which is worth knowing before reading chapter II as
its author's racial theory. It is not one.

Bogoras's own use is comparative and, in the main, descriptive — eye shape, the
bridge of the nose, the amount of body hair, always to say that the Chukchee are
less distinguishable from Europeans than his readers would expect. It does not
stay descriptive. chukchi-3-060 explains the childlessness of Russian–Chukchee marriages
by "the exhaustion of vital force," with the scarcity of "blood really Russian
and Caucasian" offered as the reason, and that is degeneration theory, not
observation. The passage is worth reading as evidence about its author.

The actual Mongols are [[people:mongols]], a different entry for a different
thing that the book spells the same way.
